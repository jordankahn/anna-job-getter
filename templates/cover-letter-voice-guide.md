# Cover Letter Voice Guide — Anna Brown

Derived from Anna's real cover letters (Chicago Public Schools / Fine & Performing
Arts Program Administrator; Chicago Shakespeare Theatre / Education Programs
Associate). Goal: every generated letter reads like **Anna wrote it** — warm,
earnest, specific — and passes as human.

## Her actual voice (do this)
- **Warm and direct first person.** Opens with genuine enthusiasm: "I am excited
  to apply for…" / "I am writing to express my interest in…".
- **Earnest, slightly personal.** She connects the role to her own story:
  "My early experiences in the arts were highly impacted by Chicago's accessible
  arts education programs." Keep a sincere, human note like this near the end.
- **Concrete proof, not adjectives.** She names real duties and numbers
  (co-director of the Jewish Chicago Film Festival; Unit Head for 80+ campers and
  16 counselors). Always tie a claim to a specific role.
- **Plain, mid-length sentences.** Mostly compound sentences joined with "and"
  or "as well as." Some sentences run a little long; that is fine and natural.
- **Mission-forward.** She speaks to access, equity, students, community, and
  "meaningful opportunities." Mirror the org's mission language honestly.
- **Standard, polite scaffolding.** "Dear Hiring Team," → 3–4 body paragraphs →
  "Thank you for your time and consideration." → "Sincerely, Anna Brown" with
  email + phone beneath.
- **Structure she uses:** (1) hook + headline value; (2) current role evidence
  mapped to the posting's responsibilities; (3) education + a second proof point;
  (4) why *this* org / personal pull + close.

## Recurring phrases that sound like her (use sparingly, vary them)
- "I am excited to apply for…" / "I am writing to express my interest in…"
- "I bring a strong commitment to…"
- "This experience aligns closely with…"
- "I am drawn to this position because…"
- "I would be honored to contribute…"
- "Thank you for your time and consideration."
- "I look forward to the opportunity to discuss…"

## Hard bans — AI tells (never use)
- **No em dashes (—) at all.** Use a comma, "and," or a period instead. (En dashes
  for numeric ranges like "grades 5–8" are acceptable, matching her own usage.)
- No "delve," "tapestry," "testament to," "underscores," "navigate the landscape,"
  "in today's fast-paced world," "robust," "leverage," "boasts," "spearheaded,"
  "passionate about leveraging," "at the intersection of," "ever-evolving,"
  "fast-paced," "synergy," "holistic," "dynamic" (as filler).
- No "It's not just X, it's Y" constructions. No rule-of-three triplet stacking
  in every sentence. No "Furthermore/Moreover/Additionally" pile-ups (she uses
  "Additionally" at most once).
- No over-formal throat-clearing ("I am reaching out to…"), no buzzword salad,
  no hollow superlatives ("world-class," "cutting-edge").
- No fabricated specifics. Every concrete claim must trace to the master profile.

## Style checklist (run before finalizing)
1. Zero em dashes? (search the text for "—")
2. Does it name at least two specific, true accomplishments from her history?
3. Does it explicitly mirror 3–5 responsibilities/keywords from the posting?
4. Is there one sincere, personal "why this org" line?
5. Sentence rhythm varied, not uniform? Any triplet stacking removed?
6. Reads aloud like a real, warm person — not a template?
7. One page, ~300–400 words, "Dear Hiring Team," + "Sincerely, Anna Brown" + contact block.

## Only when required
Generate a cover letter **only if the posting asks for one** (or it is clearly
expected). If optional, note that in the evaluation and skip unless requested.
